Before You Enter the Map

We are all descendants of global indigenous societies. That is true for every human being on this earth. For that reason, these traditions are intended for all of us. The knowledge of our ancestors is our birthright, stolen from us by the destruction bred from centuries of colonization and globalization. The intent of these maps is not to appropriate any traditions or claim precise expert knowledge, but rather as a starting point for users to get back in touch with their own ancestral traditions.

For most of human history, people lived in rhythm with the sky, the land, the water, and the cycles of food and seasons. Every culture had its own ways of naming and remembering those rhythms through the phases of the moons, stars, rains, migrations, and harvests. Colonization, industrialization, and assimilation tore many of those threads away, and often created a narrative of violence, a pernicious whispering that such knowledge was “primitive” or unimportant.

This work is both an act of reclamation and of solidarity. These traditions were ours, are still ours, and can still be reclaimed for ourselves and for generations to come.

“This land was ours once, and is, and will be again.” -Gloria Anzaldúa
